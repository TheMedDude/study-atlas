### PDF page 10 — Thermodynamic quantities describing energy changes

Classification: teaching. Printed slide: not visible.

#### Faithful slide text

```text
Dr. Osama Essawi 2025
               Thermodynamic quantities describing energy changes



⬣    Free energy (ΔG) is the energy that can perform work during a reaction.
            - Exergonic reactions release free energy (ΔG < 0)
            - Endergonic reactions gain free energy (ΔG > 0).

⬣    Enthalpy (ΔH) is the heat content of the reacting system.
            - Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0)
            - Endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH > 0).

⬣    Entropy (ΔS) is a measure of randomness or disorder in a system. Reactions
     with a gain in entropy involve more disordered products.

⬣    The relationship between ΔG, ΔH, and ΔS is given by the equation
                                   ΔG = ΔH –      TΔS
```

#### Figures, tables and reading details

Graph axes: potential energy of molecules versus progress of reaction. Exergonic graph has higher reactant than product energy and shows energy output; endergonic graph has higher product than reactant energy and shows energy input.

#### Indexed relationships

- **Free energy and reaction sign** (core; `bioenergetics-and-metabolism-gibbs-sign`): Free energy ΔG is energy capable of performing work. Exergonic reactions release free energy (ΔG < 0); endergonic reactions gain free energy (ΔG > 0).
- **Enthalpy and heat sign** (core; `bioenergetics-and-metabolism-enthalpy-sign`): Enthalpy ΔH is the heat content of the reacting system. Exothermic reactions release heat (ΔH < 0); endothermic reactions absorb heat (ΔH > 0).
- **Entropy** (core; `bioenergetics-and-metabolism-entropy`): Entropy ΔS measures randomness/disorder. Gain of entropy corresponds to more disordered products.
- **Gibbs energy equation** (core; `bioenergetics-and-metabolism-gibbs-equation`): ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.

            Citric Acid Cycle


⬣   In each cycle turn, one acetyl group enters

⬣   One molecule of oxaloacetate is used to

⬣   Four of the eight steps involve oxidations,

#### Figure, image and layout transcription

Full labeled cycle: 1 aldol condensation; 2a dehydration and 2b rehydration reposition citrate OH in isocitrate to set up next decarboxylation; 3 oxidative decarboxylation, OH oxidized to carbonyl and CO2 lost; 4 oxidative decarboxylation with PDH-like mechanism dependent on carbonyl on adjacent carbon; 5 substrate-level phosphorylation, thioester energy conserved in GTP/ATP phosphoanhydride; 6 dehydrogenation introduces double bond and initiates methylene oxidation sequence; 7 hydration introduces OH for next oxidation; 8 OH dehydrogenation gives carbonyl positioned for aldol condensation.
