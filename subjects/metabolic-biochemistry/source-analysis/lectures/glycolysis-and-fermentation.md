## PDF page 5 — Cellular Respiration (Glucose Catabolism)

> Source: supplied Chapter 14 PDF, PDF page 5. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

### Preserved slide text

Dr. Osama Essawi 2025
             Cellular Respiration (Glucose Catabolism)

  ⬣    Cellular respiration, is a multi-step process that converts glucose into energy (ATP)
       through different biochemical pathways. It consists of four main stages:

1. Glycolysis (Anaerobic Respiration)
-   Occurs in the cytoplasm of the cell.
-   Break 1 glucose molecule into 2 pyruvate molecules.
-   Produces a net of 2 ATP and 2 NADH molecules.

2. Formation of Acetyl Coenzyme A
-   Occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
-   Each pyruvate (C₃) is converted into acetyl-CoA (C₂)
-   Generates 2 NADH from NAD⁺.

3. Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle) –  Aerobic Process
-   Takes place in the mitochondrial matrix.
-   Produces a net of 2 ATP, 6 NADH and 2 FADH₂  molecules.

4. Electron Transport Chain (ETC) -    Oxidative Phosphorylation
-   Occurs in the inner mitochondrial membrane.
-   NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the ETC generating about 32-34 ATP per glucose.

Oxidative phosphorylation (electron transport and chemiosmosis): +about 28 ATP. Maximum per glucose: about 32 ATP. Cytosolic NADH shuttle labels show 2 NADH or 2 FADH2 routes.
