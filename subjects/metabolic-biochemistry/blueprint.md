## How these facts behave

ATP, phosphate transfer, redox carriers and enzyme regulation recur across all four files. The difficulty lies in distinguishing the roles of closely related quantities and molecules: ΔG versus ΔG′°, equilibrium versus rate, NAD+ versus NADH and FAD versus FADH2, ATP consumption versus substrate-level ATP production, glycolysis versus gluconeogenesis, and electron transfer versus ATP synthesis. Different compartments matter: cytosol, mitochondrial matrix and the two sides of the inner membrane. Different denominators matter as well: one reaction, one acetyl-CoA and one glucose cannot be interchanged.

Sequences are useful where the diagrams explicitly teach them: glycolysis, PDH enzyme/coenzyme transfers, the TCA cycle, and electron routes from I or II through mobile carriers. Branches and shuttles must retain their parallel routes; do not force a single unique order onto them. Classifications include metabolic pathway shapes, biochemical reaction categories, electron-carrier classes, and fuel/predecessor entry points. Regulatory diagrams contrast ATP-rich/NADH-rich conditions with ADP/AMP/NAD+-rich demand. Questions should make the condition explicit rather than assume an unstated cell, tissue or nutritional state.

Numbers are selective aids to relationships, not a memorization quota. The sources contain inconsistent ATP totals, an ambiguous mass-action-ratio typography, differing hexokinase Km values and questionable reaction/table labels. Preserve each, flag it and block the doubtful claim from deciding an answer. Do not resolve those differences from general biochemistry. Source tables are a separate owner-selection gate, described below.
