What is a Parasite?

-An organism that grows, feeds, and lives on or in a host,
benefiting from the host while providing no benefit to its

## PDF page 8 — LD50 and ID50

> **Source:** 5) Bacterial Pathogenesis.pdf, PDF page 8.

### Written content

INFECTIOUS DOSE & VIRULENCE (LD₅₀ and ID₅₀)

-LD₅₀ (50% lethal dose): number of organisms required to kill 50%
of exposed hosts.

-ID₅₀ (50% infectious dose): number of organisms required to cause

Lower LD₅₀ or ID₅₀ means greater virulence, because fewer

## PDF page 22 — Clinical states and carriers

> **Source:** 5) Bacterial Pathogenesis.pdf, PDF page 22.

### Written content

Clinical States of Infection
Inapparent (Subclinical) infection: An infection that produces no
obvious symptoms and may be detected only by:
- A rise in antibody titer, or
- Isolation/detection of the organism.

Latent infection: A state in which the pathogen remains in the host
in a dormant or inactive form and may reactivate later, causing
recurrence of symptoms.
Example: Herpes simplex virus

Chronic infection: An infection that persists for weeks, months, or
longer, with the pathogen continuing to be present and potentially
produced or shed by the host.
Chronic carrier state: The pathogen persists and may continue to
be shed, with or without symptoms.
Example: Salmonella Typhi carrier state (“Typhoid Mary”)
Chronic carriers (important source of infection)

## PDF page 24 — Generalized stages of bacterial pathogenesis

> **Source:** 5) Bacterial Pathogenesis.pdf, PDF page 24.

### Written content

STAGES OF BACTERIAL PATHOGENESIS
A generalized sequence of the stages of infection is as follows:

1- Transmission from an external source into the portal of entry.

2- Evasion of primary host defenses such as skin or stomach acid.

3- Adherence to mucous membranes, (pili, fimbriae, or adhesins).

4- Colonization by growth of the bacteria at the site of adherence.

5- Disease symptoms caused by toxin or invasion accompanied by
inflammation.

6- Host responses, both nonspecific and specific (immunity).

7- Progression, persistence, or resolution of the infection and
disease.

## PDF page 39 — Biofilms

> **Source:** 5) Bacterial Pathogenesis.pdf, PDF page 39.

### Written content

Biofilms:

-Are structured communities of bacteria embedded in a
matrix composed mainly of polysaccharides, proteins, and
extracellular DNA, formed after bacterial attachment to a
surface.

-Protect bacteria from antibiotics and host immune defenses,
including antibodies and neutrophils.

-Contribute to chronic and persistent infections.
Examples:
Pseudomonas aeruginosa biofilms contribute to persistent infection in
the lungs of cystic fibrosis patients.
Biofilms are an important component of dental plaque, which contributes

## PDF page 58 — Pathogenicity islands

> **Source:** 5) Bacterial Pathogenesis.pdf, PDF page 58.

### Written content

Pathogenicity Islands: Genetic Basis of Bacterial Virulence

-Pathogenicity islands (PAIs): Clusters of genes encoding multiple
virulence factors on the bacterial chromosome.

-Genes encoding adhesins, invasins, and exotoxins are located
adjacent to each other on these islands.

-Nonpathogenic variants of these bacteria lack pathogenicity
islands. These large genomic regions appear to have been
transferred as a block via conjugation or transduction.

-Pathogenicity islands are found in many Gram-negative rods,
such as E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella, Pseudomonas, and Vibrio
cholerae, and Gram-positive cocci, such as S. pneumoniae.
