### PDF page 6 — Main divisions of pharmacology

> Source: [original PDF](../sources/slides/01-introduction-and-roa.pdf#page=6); printed slide: 6.

Pharmacology is mainly divided into:

1.  Pharmacokinetics (PK): Study of the absorption,
    distribution metabolism and excretion (ADME) of drugs
    (“ What the body does to a drug ”)
2.  Pharmacodynamics (PD): The study of the biological
    and therapeutic effects of drugs (“What the drug does to
    the body”)
3.  Pharmacotherapeutics: It deals with the proper
    selection and use of drugs for the prevention and
    treatment of disease
4.  Toxicology: Toxicology is the branch of pharmacology
    dealing with the "undesirable" effects of drugs on
    biological processes

### PDF page 2 — Definition of pharmacokinetics

> **Source:** [Original PDF](../sources/slides/02-pharmacokinetics.pdf#page=2); PDF page 2. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

- Pharmacokinetics is derived from pharmaco (drug) and kinesis (movement).
- It is what the body does to the drug.
- It includes absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion of a drug (ADME).

### PDF page 3 — Why study pharmacokinetics?

> **Source:** [Original PDF](../sources/slides/02-pharmacokinetics.pdf#page=3); PDF page 3. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

- Pharmacokinetic properties determine onset of drug action, intensity of the drug's effect and duration of drug action.
- This permits design and optimization of treatment regimens: route of administration; dose and frequency of each dose; duration of treatment.

### PDF page 4 — Pharmacodynamic versus pharmacokinetic relationships

> **Source:** [Original PDF](../sources/slides/02-pharmacokinetics.pdf#page=4); PDF page 4. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

- Diagram: Drug administered -> Absorption -> Drug concentration in systemic circulation. Systemic concentration exchanges bidirectionally with Drug in tissues of distribution (Distribution) and Drug metabolized (Elimination).
- Systemic concentration -> Drug concentration at site of action -> Pharmacological effect -> Clinical response -> Toxicity / Efficacy. The upper bracket is Pharmacokinetics; the lower bracket is Pharmacodynamics.
