## PDF page 6 — Main divisions of pharmacology

> Source: [original PDF](../../sources/slides/01-introduction-and-roa.pdf#page=6); printed slide: 6.

Pharmacology is mainly divided into:

1.  Pharmacokinetics (PK): Study of the absorption,
    distribution metabolism and excretion (ADME) of drugs
    (“ What the body does to a drug ”)
2.  Pharmacodynamics (PD): The study of the biological
    and therapeutic effects of drugs (“What the drug does to
    the body”)
3.  Pharmacotherapeutics: It deals with the proper
    selection and use of drugs for the prevention and
    treatment of disease
4.  Toxicology: Toxicology is the branch of pharmacology
    dealing with the "undesirable" effects of drugs on
    biological processes

## PDF page 28 — First-pass effect

> Source: [original PDF](../../sources/slides/01-introduction-and-roa.pdf#page=28); printed slide: not shown.

First-pass Effect

• The  first-pass  effect  is  the  term  used              the
  intestinal  and  hepatic  degradation  or  alteration
  of  a  drug  or  substance  taken  by  mouth,  after
  absorption,    removing    some    of    the    active
  substance  from  the  blood  before  it  enters  the
  general circulation

• The greater the first-pass effect, the less the
  agent   will   reach   the   systemic   circulation
  when the agent is administered orally

## PDF page 41 — Depot preparations

> Source: [original PDF](../../sources/slides/01-introduction-and-roa.pdf#page=41); printed slide: 43.

Depot preparations:
• Often consist of a suspension of the drug
  in a non-aqueous vehicle such as
  polyethylene glycol
• Vehicle diffuses out of the muscle, the
  drug precipitates at the site of injection
• The drug then dissolves slowly, providing
  a sustained dose over an extended period
  of time

> **Source note:** Depot vehicle precipitation/dissolution is a described preparation mechanism, not evidence that every IM preparation follows it.
