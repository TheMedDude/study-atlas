## PDF page 14 - Dideoxies block elongation


> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 14](../../sources/slides/06-techniques.pdf#page=14). **Printed slide:** not shown. **Classification:** teaching.


**ddNTPs** are modified nucleotides. When incorporated into a sequence they prevent addition of further nucleotides: a phosphodiester bond cannot form between the dideoxynucleotide and the next incoming nucleotide, so the DNA chain terminates.

| Molecule shown | Structural distinction emphasized | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphate (dNTP)** | **3′ OH** | 3′ OH can be used for a phosphodiester bond. |
| **Dideoxyribonucleoside triphosphate (ddNTP)** | **No 3′ OH** (3′ H shown) | DNA synthesis terminates. |

The slide notes that **DNA polymerase incorporates nucleoside monophosphates in both cases**.


**Visual reconstruction / check:** Chemical structures and 3′ OH versus 3′ H highlighting inspected; table preserves exact causal comparison.

## PDF page 23 - Restriction enzymes


> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 23](../../sources/slides/06-techniques.pdf#page=23). **Printed slide:** not shown. **Classification:** teaching.


Restriction enzymes are **DNA-cutting enzymes found in bacteria** and harvested from them for use. Because they cut **within the molecule**, they are called **restriction endonucleases**.

Different bacterial species make different restriction nucleases. They protect bacteria from viruses by **degrading foreign incoming viral DNA**.

Recognition sequences in the bacterium’s own genome are protected from cleavage by **methylation at A/C residues**, as stated on the slide.
