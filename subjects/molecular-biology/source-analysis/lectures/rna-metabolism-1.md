## PDF page 7 - DNA-dependent synthesis of RNA

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 7. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

RNA polymerase adds ribonucleotide units to the **3'-hydroxyl end**, building RNA **5' to 3'**.

- Nucleotides are selected by Watson-Crick pairing; base-pair geometry contributes to selection.
- The 3' OH at the active site acts as nucleophile attacking the phosphate of incoming **rNTP**.
- Figure labels: **transcription bubble**, **nontemplate strand**, **template strand**, **RNA polymerase**, **active site**, **RNA-DNA hybrid, 8 bp**, emerging RNA 5' end.
- In the depicted left-to-right transcription direction, DNA **unwinds ahead** at right and **rewinds behind** at left.

## PDF page 8 - Stages of transcription

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 8. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

- **Initiation:** starts at a promoter; RNA polymerase binds specific DNA sequences.
- **Elongation:** growing RNA temporarily pairs with the template as a short **8 bp RNA-DNA hybrid**; hybrid peels off and the DNA duplex reforms. Rate: **50-90 nucleotides/s**.
- **Termination:** named as the third stage; mechanisms follow on later pages.

## PDF page 9 - Transcription and DNA supercoiling

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 9. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

Because DNA is helical, transcription-bubble movement requires strand rotation, causing supercoiling in vitro and in vivo; **topoisomerase** relieves it.

The left-to-right figure depicts **negative supercoils behind** the transcription complex and **positive supercoils ahead**, labels emerging RNA 5' end, and annotates **17 bp unwound, 8 bp DNA-RNA hybrid**.

## PDF page 17 - Factor-independent and Rho-dependent termination

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 17. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

**Factor independent:**

- A **GC-rich transcript segment** is stated to make the template hard to unwind and slow RNA polymerase.
- Weak **A=U bonds** cause further weakening/dissociation, collapsing the transcription bubble.

**Rho (rho) factor dependent:**

- Rho loads at specific mRNA-binding sites and moves **5' to 3'** toward the transcription complex.
- It proceeds along mRNA and probably outraces/catches up to RNA polymerase, as worded.
- Its **ATP-dependent helicase activity** disrupts the RNA-DNA hybrid.
- Termination completes with release of Rho and RNA polymerase from nucleic acids.

> **rna1-gc-wording - assessment blocked for this claim:** P17 explains slowing as GC pairs making the template hard to unwind, while pp18-19 depict a transcript hairpin. Preserve both; block the precise causal unwinding explanation rather than silently repairing it.

## PDF page 30 - Three eukaryotic mRNA modifications

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 30. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

1. **Capping:** modified **5' cap** residue is added at the 5' end before transcription ends.
2. **Splicing:** introns are removed and exons joined. **Alternative splicing** allows some transcripts to be spliced in more than one way and has regulatory significance.
3. **Polyadenylation:** the 3' end is cleaved and **80-250 A residues** are added as a **poly(A) tail**.

The source states these modifications work together with **phosphorylated CTD and RNA polymerase II**. The list enumerates modification types and does not establish a unique chronological order for all three processes.

## PDF page 31 - Primary transcript to mature mRNA diagram

> Source: `rna-metabolism-1`, PDF page 31. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

The diagram explicitly connects these stages:

1. **Transcription and 5' capping:** RNA polymerase moves along DNA, leaving emerging RNA labeled **5' cap**, **exon** and **intron**.
2. **Completion of primary transcript:** a **primary transcript** contains the cap, alternating exon/intron segments, and a **noncoding end sequence** toward the **3'** end.
3. **Cleavage, polyadenylation, and splicing:** introns loop out and are removed; dashed guides show retained exon segments joining, while end cleavage and poly(A) addition are grouped with splicing.

These grouped operations do not justify requiring an arbitrary unique order among cleavage, polyadenylation and individual splicing events.
