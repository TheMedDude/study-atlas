## PDF page 3 - Mutation: base changes and product effects

> Source: `dna-metabolism-2`, PDF page 3. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

A mutation is a **permanent change in the nucleotide sequence**. The slide classifies mutations by two aspects:

1. **Nature of the base change:**
   - Substitution: replacement of one or more base pairs with another.
   - Insertion/deletion: addition/deletion of one or more base pairs.
2. **Effect on coding sequence and product:**
   - Silent: affects nonessential DNA or has negligible effect, as worded in the source.
   - Nonsense: premature stop codon.
   - Missense: change in the amino acid encoded.

## PDF page 11 - Base-excision repair (BER)

> Source: `dna-metabolism-2`, PDF page 11. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

- **DNA glycosylases** recognize lesions, including A/C-deamination products and lesions caused by environmental agents.
- Removal of a base cleaves the **N-glycosyl bond between base and deoxyribose**, leaving an **apurinic/apyrimidinic (abasic, AP) site**.
- Once an AP site forms it must be repaired.
- **AP endonuclease** cuts the DNA strand containing the AP site.
- The AP-containing segment is removed; **DNA polymerase I** replaces DNA and **ligase** seals the nick.

The four-part diagram shows (a) damaged-base release by glycosylase, (b) AP-endonuclease incision, (c) DNA pol I with NTPs as printed, release of deoxyribose phosphate plus dNMPs, new DNA and a nick, and (d) ligase sealing.

## PDF page 21 - Three types of DNA recombination

> Source: `dna-metabolism-2`, PDF page 21. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

DNA recombination: movement of a DNA segment from one DNA molecule to another.

1. **Homologous/general recombination/DNA crossover:** between two homologous molecules.
2. **Site-specific recombination:** at a particular sequence in both nonhomologous molecules.
3. **Transpositional recombination:** a mobile element/short segment inserts into target DNA.

## PDF page 26 - Double-strand break repair system

> Source: `dna-metabolism-2`, PDF page 26. Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

Most damage is described as BER/NER-repaired, but replication forks encounter breaks. Two homologous chromosomes participate:

1. A double-strand break becomes a gap through exonuclease action. More removal at **5' ends** leaves **3' single-stranded extensions**; the diagram says strands with 3' ends degrade less than strands with 5' ends.
2. An exposed 3' end invades and pairs with its complement in an intact homolog, displacing the other duplex strand.
3. DNA polymerase extends the invading 3' end; **branch migration** yields two crossovers/**Holliday intermediates**.
4. Further replication replaces DNA missing from the original break.
5. Specialized nucleases cleave the intermediates, producing **either of two joining products**.

The diagram labels **Gene A/Gene B**, strand ends, and **product set 1/product set 2**. In product set 2, DNA on either side of the repaired region is recombined. These are alternative outcomes, not one uniquely required product.
