## PDF page 3 - Semiconservative replication and Meselson-Stahl

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 3](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=3). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

Characteristic 1: **Semiconservative replication**. As revealed by the Watson-Crick model, one DNA strand serves as a template for replication.

Meselson-Stahl experiment:

1. E. coli grown in medium containing only **15N**, the heavy nitrogen isotope, as its nitrogen source.
2. Cells transferred to medium containing only **14N**, the lighter, normal, more abundant nitrogen isotope. After the population doubled, isolated DNA showed **one hybrid band**.
3. After growth in fresh 14N medium, isolated DNA showed **two bands**.

**Visual review:** DNA extracted and centrifuged to equilibrium in a **CsCl density gradient**. Original parent = heavy DNA (15N). First-generation daughters = two hybrid molecules (15N/14N), one band. Second-generation daughters = two hybrid molecules + two light molecules; bands labeled light DNA (14N) and hybrid DNA. Strand colors distinguish inherited and newly synthesized DNA.

## PDF page 11 - DNA polymerization reaction

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 11](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=11). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

DNA is synthesized by DNA polymerase. Kornberg's group purified/characterized **E. coli DNA polymerase I**, encoded by **polA**; later studies found four other E. coli DNA polymerases.

The fundamental reaction is phosphoryl-group transfer. The nucleophile is the growing strand's **3'-OH**, which attacks the **alpha phosphorus** of an incoming deoxynucleoside 5'-triphosphate.

**(dNMP)n + dNTP -> (dNMP)n+1 + PPi**

**Visual review:** Requirements labeled: (1) template DNA strand with a single unpaired region; (2) growing DNA strand/primer supplying the 3'-OH (primer terminus). Incoming deoxynucleoside 5'-triphosphate is selected by base pairing. The drawing adds T opposite template A to a growing strand ending A-G; template displayed T-C-A-G. **Inorganic pyrophosphate** leaves. The resulting base pairing and stacking stabilize the product. The figure labels deoxyribose, 3'/5' polarity, and phosphate (P). Text beneath: DNA polymerase involves **two Mg2+ ions**; **Asp is conserved in all DNA polymerases**.

## PDF page 12 - Base pairing and processivity

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 12](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=12). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

DNA polymerization is guided by base pairing: A in the template leads to addition of T. The slide calls this the first example of a template leading a reaction.

A primer means part of the product/new strand is already present. All DNA polymerases require a preexisting primer; RNA polymerase synthesizes a primer when required.

After adding a nucleotide, the polymerase can dissociate or move along the template and add more. Dissociation/reassociation affects and limits the polymerization rate. (The text says "DNA either dissociates/moves" in this sentence; the following sentence explicitly names polymerase.)

**Processivity** = average number of nucleotides added before dissociation. Some enzymes add only a few, others thousands.

**Visual review:** A-T and C-G structural drawings are labeled "Similar Geometry" and "fit in active site." A-T has two drawn hydrogen bonds; C-G has three.

## PDF page 13 - Replication accuracy and nucleotide selection

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 13](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=13). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

E. coli replication is stated to make **one mistake per 10^9-10^10 nucleotides**, or one mistake every **1,000-10,000 replications**.

Discrimination between correct and incorrect nucleotides:

1. Hydrogen bonding.
2. Common geometry of standard A-T and G-C pairs; the polymerase active site accommodates correct geometry.

Incorrect bases are rejected before phosphodiester-bond formation. This alone does not explain the observed fidelity: studies showed an incorrect base added per **10^4-10^5 correct bases**. The slide introduces the need for another mechanism.

**Visual review:** Drawings of nonstandard G-A, C-A, and T-G pairings illustrate wrong geometry, annotated as excluded from the active site. Exact atomic drawings are retained in the original PDF; no new mismatch frequencies are supplied.

## PDF page 14 - Proofreading

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 14](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=14). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

Heading as printed: "Intrinsic property of all polymerases."

**3' -> 5' exonuclease activity** checks each nucleotide after addition: **proofreading**. An incorrect/mismatched base inhibits polymerase translocation, producing a **kinetic pause**, an opportunity for correction. The exonuclease removes the mismatch, then polymerization resumes. Proofreading is not reversal of polymerization; **pyrophosphate is not involved**.

Proofreading improves accuracy to **one error per 10^6-10^8 bases added**. Observed replication accuracy is still higher, so the lecture introduces a **repair system**.

**Visual review:** DNA polymerase I has separately labeled polymerase and 3' -> 5' proofreading exonuclease active sites. The full figure sequence:

1. A rare tautomeric form of cytosine (**C***), which pairs with A, is incorporated into the growing strand.
2. Before the polymerase moves on, C* shifts back to C; the new nucleotide is now mispaired.
3. The mispaired 3'-OH blocks elongation; polymerase slides back to position the mismatch in the exonuclease active site.
4. The mispaired nucleotide is removed.
5. Polymerase slides forward and resumes polymerization.

> **Source uncertainty (dna-metabolism-1-u14-all-polymerases):** The heading makes proofreading universal, but page 26 explicitly states that DNA polymerase α has no proofreading 3' -> 5' exonuclease activity. Preserve both. Do not assess the universal "all polymerases" claim.

## PDF page 24 - Termination, Tus and Ter

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 24](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=24). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.

## PDF page 25 - Catenanes and topoisomerase IV

> **Source:** [Original PDF, page 25](../../sources/slides/02-dna-metabolism-1.pdf#page=25). Printed slide: not shown. Classification: teaching.
